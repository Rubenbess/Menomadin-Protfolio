## Section 1: Top Deals

**Mave** | Series A (+Seed) | $34M Total ($28M Series A + $6M Seed)
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber, AI]
- What they do: AI-powered SecOps platform that embeds autonomous agents into existing enterprise security stacks. Agents investigate alerts, filter false positives, and execute approved response actions without moving data to a separate system.
- Investors: Red Dot Capital Partners led the Series A. Dell Technologies Capital and Emerge Ventures led the earlier seed. Lumir Growth Partners co-invested in the Series A.
- Why it matters: This is the largest VC round of the week. Mave addresses the security operations bottleneck where alert fatigue and analyst shortage degrade response times. The agentic approach integrates with deployed tools rather than replacing them, which lowers adoption friction.
- Signals: Founders Raziel Oren (CEO) and Guy Eshel (CTO) are Unit 8200 alumni. The company emerged from stealth this week with 30 employees and claims 400%+ ARR growth (company-stated, unverified). Red Dot's lead suggests conviction in the go-to-market.
- Suggested angle: Catalyst mandate. Cyber/AI. The Series A just closed, so Series B is likely 12-18 months out. Track for potential Catalyst investment at next stage. The SecOps automation market is heating up fast.

**Elementary / Boomi** | M&A | ~$40-45M (estimated, cash + stock)
- What they do: Data observability platform that detects and fixes data quality issues before they reach dashboards, ML models, and AI agents. Offers column-level lineage and an MCP server for AI tool integration. Over 1,500 data teams and 1.1M+ monthly open-source downloads.
- Acquirer: Boomi, a data activation platform. Second acquisition after Lunar.dev. All 17 employees join Boomi.
- Why it matters: Validates the data observability category as essential infrastructure for the AI era. Open-source traction (1.1M downloads) built a wide top-of-funnel that converted into acquisition interest.
- Signals: The deal closed quickly relative to Elementary's scale. The open-source-to-acquisition pathway continues to work for Israeli data infrastructure companies.
- Suggested angle: Monitor data quality and observability startups in Israel. The category is consolidating, and other platforms (dbt, Monte Carlo, Soda) may be acquisition targets.

**Two Delta** | Seed | Undisclosed (tens of millions)
[MANDATE FIT: AI, Deeptech]
- What they do: Building technology to accelerate AI model inference. Claims 2x average throughput vs. standard decoding and 19.8% improvement over the competing DFlash method in tests across 10 datasets (company-reported benchmarks).
- Investors: Bill Ackman (Pershing Square) and Eric Schmidt (former Google chairman) among backers. Exact lead investor unconfirmed.
- Why it matters: The investor names are the signal. Ackman and Schmidt rarely co-invest at seed stage. The bet is on AI inference efficiency becoming a critical scaling bottleneck as model deployment costs dominate inference budgets.
- Signals: Co-founded by Dvir Zeltzer (ex-Google senior role) and Dr. Eli David (veteran AI researcher). Fewer than 10 employees. Operating in near-total stealth with only research papers on its website.
- Suggested angle: Catalyst mandate. Deeptech/AI. Pre-revenue and deeply technical. Worth monitoring as an infrastructure play that could become a platform or acquisition target. The Ackman/Schmidt backing raises the profile considerably.

**UltraSight** | Series B2 | $24M
[MANDATE FIT: HealthTech]
- What they do: FDA-cleared AI platform (Echosystem) that guides ultrasound imaging of the heart. Non-specialist clinicians can perform cardiac scans with AI guidance. In use at 10+ commercial sites including clinical partners.
- Investors: Alive HealthTech Growth Fund led. Deep Insight, Star51 Capital, Connecticut Innovations, eHealth Ventures participated. Existing backers Mayo Clinic Ventures, NYU, and Iron Nation followed on. Total raised now $42M.
- Why it matters: The investor syndicate (Mayo Clinic, NYU) provides both capital and clinical validation pathways. FDA clearance is a hard moat. The platform has potential to democratize cardiac screening in settings without cardiologists.
- Signals: CEO Davidi Vortman leading expansion into ICU and circulatory support workflows, broadening from the initial echo-guidance use case. The Mayo Clinic follow-on is a strong endorsement of clinical utility.
- Suggested angle: Impact mandate. HealthTech. Series B2 stage may be late for Impact entry, but worth tracking for the sector signal: AI-guided diagnostics with FDA clearance and top-tier hospital validation is attracting growth capital.

**Modelyo** | Seed | ~$17M
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber]
- What they do: Sovereign Confidential Cloud platform. Lets organizations run cloud and AI workloads while keeping data encrypted during processing, with cryptographic verification that the provider never sees the data or keys.
- Investors: OlivTree (US) led. Seth Merrin and David Phelps also invested.
- Why it matters: Addresses the trust gap that prevents governments, defense agencies, banks, and healthcare organizations from using public cloud for sensitive workloads. The confidential computing market is growing as AI processing moves to the cloud.
- Signals: Founded in 2024, bootstrapped until now. About 30 employees. Claims partnerships with NVIDIA, Intel, and Google (unverified). The timing is right as sovereign cloud mandates tighten across Europe and the Middle East.
- Suggested angle: Catalyst mandate. Cyber. Seed stage, within co-investment range. The sovereign cloud thesis is regulatory-driven, which provides downside protection. Worth exploring for follow-on at Series A.

## Section 3: Market Insights

- **Quieter week by deal count, active by category depth.** Five confirmed deals above $500K, down from nine the prior week. But the deals that landed are thematically concentrated: three of five touch cybersecurity or AI infrastructure, continuing the pattern from Q3.

- **AI agent security is now a three-company race in Israel.** With Mave ($34M) joining Pluto Security ($42M, prior week) and Rein Security ($25M, Oct 8), three Israeli startups have collectively raised over $100M for AI agent and SecOps automation in October alone. Each approaches the problem differently: Mave automates SOC workflows, Pluto secures shadow AI tools, Rein protects agents at runtime. Expect consolidation or further specialization.

- **AI inference infrastructure attracting elite capital.** Two Delta's marquee investor list (Ackman, Schmidt) and Axionic's $80M pre-seed (prior week) suggest that AI inference efficiency is becoming a distinct investment category. Both companies are pre-revenue and deeply technical, which signals that investors are making long-horizon bets on the next layer of AI infrastructure.

- **Israeli VC ecosystem health signal.** Vintage Investment Partners closed $920M, its largest raise ever. As one of the most prolific Israeli fund-of-funds managers, this signals sustained LP confidence in the Israeli venture ecosystem despite geopolitical volatility.

- **M&A pipeline remains active.** The Boomi/Elementary deal follows three exits the prior week (Varonis $6.3B, Geniez AI ~$100M, Loops ~$30M). Israeli data infrastructure companies continue to attract platform acquirers looking to embed observability and AI capabilities.

Seed stage is within co-investment range. The team bootstrapped for two years before raising, which suggests capital discipline. Series A co-investment is the most likely entry point.
