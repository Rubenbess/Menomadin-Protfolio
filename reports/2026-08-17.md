## Section 1: Top Deals

**Corma** | Seed | $60M
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber, AI]
- What they do: Tel Aviv and San Francisco-based startup building proprietary foundation models purpose-built for defensive cybersecurity. Deploys AI agents across an organization's existing security stack to carry out detection and response tasks end-to-end, replacing dashboard-centric workflows.
- Investors: Sequoia Capital (lead), Khosla Ventures, Coatue
- Why it matters: This is one of the largest seed rounds globally in 2026 and the first time Sequoia, Khosla, and Coatue have co-invested in a single Israeli seed deal. Early deployments reduced threat-response times by 94% and expanded security coverage by 15x. The founding team combines Google/DeepMind AI researchers with Unit 8200 cybersecurity veterans.
- Signals: Three top-tier US funds in a seed round reflects extreme conviction. Corma is already working with Fortune 100 and Fortune 500 clients, unusual at seed stage. The "foundation model for defense" thesis is distinct from the crowded point-solution cyber market.
- Suggested angle: This company sits squarely in the Catalyst mandate at the intersection of AI and Cyber. The $60M seed size likely puts direct investment out of range, but understanding their platform architecture matters for evaluating adjacent plays in agentic security operations and defensive AI tooling.

**Anthropic / Decart** | M&A (In Talks) | ~$6B
- What they do: Decart builds software that reduces the cost of training and operating AI by optimizing how chips execute inference. Also develops world models that simulate physical environments for applications like autonomous driving.
- Acquirer (reported): Anthropic
- Why it matters: If finalized, this would be Anthropic's largest acquisition and one of the biggest Israeli tech exits on record. Decart raised $300M at a $4B valuation in May 2026 with Nvidia as an investor. A $6B price tag represents a 50% premium over the last round. The deal would give Anthropic a ~100-person Israeli R&D operation and access to one of the densest AI talent pools in the world.
- Signals: Bloomberg reported the talks on August 13. The deal is not confirmed and could still fall apart. If it closes, it signals that frontier AI companies are willing to pay significant premiums for inference efficiency technology. Watch for competitive acquisition moves from other foundation model companies.
- Suggested angle: Not actionable for direct investment, but the valuation benchmark is significant for portfolio companies and pipeline deals in AI infrastructure and deeptech. Israeli AI infrastructure talent may become scarcer post-acquisition.

## Section 3: Market Insights

- **A notably quieter funding week.** After last week's concentration of nine deals totaling over $2.7B in confirmed capital, this week produced only two confirmed venture rounds (Corma and Marquee) and one pharma M&A. The contrast is sharp and may reflect seasonal patterns as August progresses, though deal flow can be lumpy.

- **The biggest story is potential M&A, not venture funding.** Anthropic's reported $6B pursuit of Decart and Wonderful's $500M fundraising ambition dominated headlines. Both remain unconfirmed, but if either closes, it would rank among the top Israeli tech transactions of 2026.

- **Sequoia Capital planted a flag in Israeli cybersecurity.** Corma's $60M seed led by Sequoia, with Khosla and Coatue, is the week's only confirmed major venture deal but carries outsized signal value. Sequoia rarely leads Israeli seeds at this scale. The "foundation model for cyber defense" thesis marks a new category within the already-crowded Israeli cyber ecosystem.

- **Ecosystem capital continues to flow.** Team8's $365M raise and the Ofir-SouthUp $20-30M regional fund both reflect confidence in the Israeli startup pipeline. Team8 in particular is a bellwether: its LP roster of enterprise buyers (Walmart, Cisco, Moody's) signals that strategic investors view Israeli AI-native companies as core to their technology roadmaps.

- **The Menomadin fund was among investors in Voltify's $30M seed** (announced earlier in 2026), reflecting the firm's active deployment in CleanTech/Energy. This aligns with the Impact mandate's positioning in climate infrastructure.
