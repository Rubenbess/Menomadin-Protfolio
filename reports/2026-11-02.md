## Section 1: Top Deals

**Upwind Security** | Series C | $400M
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber]
- What they do: Cloud and AI security platform providing runtime protection across endpoints, code, and cloud infrastructure. Reports ARR exceeding $50M and trending toward $100M by year-end.
- Investors: Bessemer Venture Partners (lead), Canapi Ventures (new), all existing investors including Craft Ventures and Greylock
- Why it matters: Largest Israeli funding round in October. Upwind reportedly turned down a ~$4B acquisition offer from Datadog, signaling strong confidence in an independent path. Valued at $3.8B, up from $1.5B in January.
- Signals: Explosive growth trajectory. Total funding now exceeds $800M. Cloud security remains the highest-conviction subsector for late-stage investors.
- Suggested angle: Too late for direct investment at this stage, but track Upwind's ecosystem. Smaller startups building adjacent capabilities (cloud-native SIEM, AI workload protection) could benefit from validated market demand.

**Intel acquires Classiq** | M&A | ~$2.3B (cash and stock)
[MANDATE FIT: Deeptech]
- What they do: Tel Aviv-based quantum computing software company specializing in circuit synthesis, enabling developers to write high-level quantum algorithms that compile efficiently to hardware.
- Acquirer: Intel, combining Classiq with its spin-qubit hardware program to build a full-stack quantum computing platform.
- Why it matters: Largest disclosed Israeli tech acquisition since Q3. Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan positioned this as transforming their quantum group from research to commercial platform. Validates quantum software as an acquirable asset class.
- Signals: Strategic buyers are paying premium multiples for quantum IP. Pending regulatory approval, expected to close H1 2027.
- Suggested angle: Monitor other Israeli quantum and deeptech companies. The Classiq exit at ~$2.3B sets a strong benchmark for the sector.

**Unconventional AI acquires NeuReality** | M&A | $150--200M (est.)
- What they do: Israeli AI chip startup developing inference-optimized processors (NR-NEXUS platform). Founded 2019, raised ~$105M to date.
- Acquirer: Unconventional AI (Naveen Rao's $4.5B-valued AI compute startup), acquiring NeuReality's ~40-person team and Israel engineering center.
- Why it matters: Demonstrates U.S. AI infrastructure companies are actively acquiring Israeli chip talent. The deal was announced the same day as the Intel/Classiq acquisition, marking an extraordinary day for Israeli deeptech exits.
- Signals: Two deeptech acquisitions on the same day (Oct 26) suggest a pattern of strategic buyers consolidating Israeli hardware/quantum talent.
- Suggested angle: Israeli AI chip and deeptech startups are in high demand. Watch for follow-on effects on talent pricing and new company formation in this space.

**Bites** | Series A | $40M
[MANDATE FIT: AI]

**Mave** | Series A | $28M ($34M total)
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber]
- What they do: Agentic SecOps platform. AI agents connect to existing security tools, investigate alerts, triage incidents, and filter false positives in real time. Tel Aviv-based, 30+ employees.
- Investors: Red Dot Capital Partners (lead), Lumir Growth Partners, Dell Technologies Capital, Emerge Ventures
- Why it matters: Reports ~400% ARR run-rate growth in six months. The agentic security operations category is attracting significant capital as enterprises struggle with alert fatigue. Competes with Dropzone AI and Prophet Security.

- Suggested angle: Series A with strong growth metrics and a well-defined category. Within Catalyst check-size range ($5M). Worth a first meeting to assess competitive positioning versus Dropzone and Prophet.

## Section 3: Market Insights

- **Cybersecurity dominance continues.** Five of the ten funding rounds this period were in cybersecurity, and two of the three M&A deals targeted security companies. Upwind's $400M round, Varonis's $6.3B exit, and the cluster of agentic security startups (Mave, Rein, Pluto) confirm that Israeli cyber remains the highest-capital-velocity sector in the ecosystem.

- **Agentic AI security is emerging as a distinct category.** Mave, Rein Security, and Pluto Security all raised significant rounds for products that secure, monitor, or govern AI agents. This is not yet a crowded space globally, but Israel is producing a disproportionate share of the startups in it. Expect consolidation or further mega-rounds within 12-18 months.

- **October was an extraordinary month for Israeli tech exits.** Intel/Classiq ($2.3B), Proofpoint/Varonis ($6.3B), and Unconventional AI/NeuReality ($150-200M) were all announced within two weeks. Strategic buyers, particularly in quantum, AI chips, and data security, are paying full multiples for Israeli IP.

- **Cyera's reported $20B valuation talks signal continued upside in data security.** While not a closed deal, The Information reported on Oct 30 that Cyera is in preliminary discussions at nearly double its last valuation. This, combined with the Varonis exit, suggests data security remains among the most valued subsectors.

- **Impact sectors showing steady activity.** BloomX ($13M, AgriTech), Phantom GI ($5M, MedTech), and Undecimal Bio ($18M, Biotech) all closed rounds in October. These are smaller than the cyber deals but represent continued investor interest in mandate-relevant sectors.

- **Bessemer Venture Partners appeared in two major deals** this period: leading Upwind's $400M Series C and participating in the Zenzap launch. Vertex Ventures also appeared in multiple rounds (Bites, earlier deals), confirming both firms as highly active in the Israeli ecosystem.

## Section 4: Special Flags

- **High-priority opportunity: Mave.** Series A at $28M with reported 400% ARR growth. Within Catalyst check-size range. The agentic SecOps space is early and Mave has strong investor backing (Dell Technologies Capital, Red Dot). Recommend reaching out this week before the round fully closes or the company begins scaling U.S. go-to-market.

- **High-priority opportunity: Bites.** $40M Series A with 161% NRR in the deskless worker training space. Vertex Ventures already in. The company sits at the intersection of AI and enterprise training, which could be argued as an Education-adjacent play under the Impact mandate. Worth a conversation to assess sector classification.

- **Competitive threat to Catalyst mandate: Agentic security clustering.** Three Israeli startups (Mave, Rein, Pluto) are raising significant rounds in AI agent security. If Menomadin has portfolio companies in adjacent security categories, these new entrants could shift competitive dynamics. Conversely, this represents a potential investment theme worth building conviction around.

- **Repeat founders and notable teams.** Mave's founders (Raziel Oren, Guy Eshel) and Pluto's team (ex-Microsoft, Palo Alto Networks, Bridgecrew, Unit 8200) are experienced operators. Cylake, which raised $245M in September via convertible note, was founded by Nir Zuk (Palo Alto Networks founder) alongside SentinelOne co-founder Ehud Shamir. The density of repeat cybersecurity founders launching new companies continues to rise.

- **Mandate-fit deal to watch: BloomX.** $13M Series A in AgriTech with Elah Fund leading and Israel Innovation Authority backing. Robotic pollination is a niche but defensible market. The company's pay-per-service model is differentiated. At $20M total funding, a follow-on or co-investment could fit the Impact mandate if the U.S. expansion thesis holds.
