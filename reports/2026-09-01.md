## Section 1: Top Deals

A notably thin week on the Israeli funding front, with one large funding round and two significant M&A transactions. The quiet follows a strong July and early August that saw deals from Oligo Security ($60M), Faye ($50M Series C), QuantHealth ($45M Series B), and Harmony ($34M Seed). Late August is historically slow as the market pauses ahead of September's return to activity.

**Alice** | Growth | $140 million
[MANDATE FIT: AI, Cyber]
- What they do: AI security platform that protects models against prompt injection, jailbreaks, and adversarial inputs. Formerly ActiveFence, rebranded in January 2026 to signal pivot from content moderation to direct AI model security.
- Investors: Apax Digital (lead), SentinelOne, Samsung Electronics
- Why it matters: ARR approaching $100M with Anthropic, Google, and Nvidia as customers. This is the clearest validation yet that AI model security is becoming a standalone enterprise category, not a feature of existing security suites.
- Signals: Valuation near $800M on $280M total raised. The customer list reads like a who's who of foundation model providers paying for third-party safety infrastructure. SentinelOne's participation suggests endpoint security incumbents are buying optionality in the AI safety stack.
- Suggested angle: Series A investors with AI or cyber mandates should track the downstream ecosystem. Alice's scale and customer base will create demand for complementary startups in AI observability, compliance tooling, and model governance.

**Nayax / IPS Group** | M&A | $350 million (all cash)
[MANDATE FIT: Fintech]
- What they do: Nayax (NASDAQ/TASE: NYAX), an Israeli payments and commerce platform, is acquiring IPS Group, a US-based smart parking technology provider managing 250,000+ parking spaces across the US, UK, Ireland, and Canada.
- Investors: N/A (strategic acquisition). Funded with cash on hand and ~$150M in new committed debt.
- Why it matters: Nayax's largest acquisition to date. IPS brings $90M+ in revenue (60%+ recurring) and deepens Nayax's footprint in embedded payments for physical infrastructure. At 17x estimated 2026 EBITDA, the price signals confidence in smart city payment rails.
- Signals: Israeli fintech companies are increasingly becoming acquirers rather than targets, using public market currency and cash flow to consolidate vertical payment niches. Expected to close Q4 2026.
- Suggested angle: The smart parking and urban mobility payments vertical is consolidating. Series A funds should watch for Israeli startups building adjacent infrastructure (EV charging payments, congestion pricing, parking data analytics) that could benefit from or be acquired by the combined entity.

---

**CropX / SCIO** | M&A | Undisclosed (estimated below $64M)
[MANDATE FIT: AgriTech]
- What they do: CropX, an Israeli-founded digital agronomy platform, acquired SCIO, a portable near-infrared spectroscopy company for agriculture and food quality analysis. This is CropX's 8th acquisition since 2020.
- Investors: N/A (strategic acquisition by CropX)
- Why it matters: CropX is assembling a full-stack agricultural intelligence platform spanning soil monitoring through harvest quality analysis. The acquisition connects growing conditions with real-world crop outcomes for the first time in a single platform.
- Signals: Disappointing exit for SCIO, which raised $64M and sold for less. But the deal validates the thesis that precision agriculture platforms need integrated quality measurement, not just input optimization. CropX's serial acquisition strategy suggests they are building toward a dominant position in digital agronomy.
- Suggested angle: The acqui-hire dynamic in agritech creates opportunity. Startups with complementary sensor or data capabilities in post-harvest, supply chain traceability, or crop insurance analytics could be targets for CropX or competitors building similar platforms.

## Section 3: Market Insights

- **Quiet week, by design.** The VC Cafe's August 28 newsletter described the week as "very quiet on the funding front, at least on the surface." Late August is the traditional pre-Rosh Hashana lull. Expect a return to volume in September as Israeli founders and investors resume deal activity after the holiday season.

- **AI security is now its own category.** Alice's $140M round at near-unicorn valuation, following Glow ($180M), Zenity ($125M), Onyx ($113M), and Above Security ($43M) earlier in 2026, confirms that AI model and agent security has graduated from a feature to a standalone market. Israeli founders dominate the category.

- **Israeli acquirers on the move.** Both deals this week featured Israeli companies as buyers (Nayax, CropX), not targets. This reflects a maturing ecosystem where public and growth-stage Israeli companies are using strong balance sheets to consolidate verticals. Visa's $2.4B BioCatch acquisition from earlier in August shows the other side: global strategics paying premium for Israeli trust infrastructure.

- **Capital concentration continues.** H1 2026 saw $8.6B raised across only ~129 rounds, a 45% increase in capital but a 35% decline in deal count versus H1 2025. The trend is clear: larger checks, fewer companies, higher bars for new raises. Seed rounds exceeding $30M (Harmony at $34M, Velocity at $27M) are becoming normalized for repeat founders.

- **IVC data point worth watching.** 57.9% of capital raised by Israeli tech companies in H1 2026 went to companies headquartered outside Israel, up from 48.9% in 2016. The "Israeli-founded but US-HQ" model continues to dominate at growth stage.

## Section 4: Special Flags

- **Repeat founders.** Alice's leadership team built ActiveFence from a content moderation tool into an AI security platform with $100M ARR before rebranding. The pivot-and-raise pattern is worth studying for portfolio companies considering strategic repositioning.

- **Competitive dynamics in mandate sectors.** CropX's acquisition-driven consolidation in AgriTech is narrowing the field for standalone agritech startups. Portfolio companies in adjacent spaces should consider whether partnering with or selling to CropX is more strategic than competing.

- **High-priority opportunity.** Alice's ecosystem creates derivative demand for Series A companies in AI governance, prompt security testing, and model compliance tooling. The gap between Alice's enterprise platform and the emerging regulatory requirements for AI model safety is exactly where a Series A fund should be looking. [MANDATE FIT: AI, Cyber]

- **Upcoming deal flow signal.** The end of the August quiet period, combined with Viola Ventures' new $250M fund targeting seed-stage AI, cyber, quantum, and defense startups, should drive a surge in announcements through September and October. Menomadin should prepare for inbound deal flow in these categories.
