## Section 1: Top Deals

**Rein Security** | Series A | $25M
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber / AI]
- What they do: Runtime security platform for enterprise AI agents. Monitors agent behavior in production and blocks harmful actions in real time.
- Investors: Co-led by Glilot Capital Partners and Sienna Venture Capital. Corner Ventures, Atlacle, and RNP Capital Advisors also participated.
- Why it matters: AI agent security is the breakout category of 2026, and Rein is one of a handful of companies tackling the runtime layer rather than the training or deployment layer. Revenue grew eightfold and customers fivefold since its January 2026 product launch.
- Signals: Founders Matan Bar-Efrat and Netanel Rubin are Unit 8200 alumni. Glilot doubled down after leading the $8M seed in January. Total raised now $35M.
- Suggested angle: Series A with explosive growth metrics and a strong second check from Glilot. The AI agent security space is consolidating around a few winners. Worth tracking for a potential Series B co-invest if the company stays on its current trajectory.

**Enso** | Series A | $15M (+ planned ~$25M in acquisitions)
[MANDATE FIT: AI / B2B SaaS]
- What they do: AI agent platform for marketing and growth. Helps companies monitor, experiment with, and optimize their presence in AI-generated answers from ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Google AI tools.
- Investors: Led by MoreTech Ventures, with NFX, Phenomen VC, and Gil Hirsch participating. Total raised: $23M.
- Why it matters: Enso is defining "Agentic Growth Hacking," a new category at the intersection of AI agents and performance marketing. - Signals: CEO Mickey Haslavsky previously co-founded RapidAPI. The acquisitions signal a hybrid model: AI agent technology layered onto traditional agency operations, which could create a defensible wedge.
- Suggested angle: The AI-native marketing stack is an underexplored theme for Israeli VCs. If the acquisitions close and the integrated model works, Enso will have both technology and distribution. Early enough to track for the next round.

**BloomX** | Series A | $13M
[MANDATE FIT: AgriTech]

- Investors: Led by Elah Fund, with participation from Rothschild, OurCrowd, Sadot, and agricultural cooperatives including Granot-affiliated kibbutzim (Magal, Horashim, Eyal).

- Signals: Kibbutz investors plan to be early adopters of the technology, providing real-world validation. The problem (pollinator decline) is structural and growing.
- Suggested angle: Strong mandate fit for the Impact track. Series A with commercial traction and a clear path to scale in new crops and regions. The agricultural co-investors provide channel access that few agritech startups have at this stage. Worth a direct conversation.

**Phantom GI** | Seed | $5M

- Suggested angle: Fits the Impact mandate at the lower end of the check range. If the clinical data holds, this becomes a compelling Series A candidate in 12-18 months. Worth tracking and potentially engaging Pitango for co-investment downstream.

## Section 3: Market Insights

- **AI agent security is the dominant theme.** Rein Security's Series A follows a pattern visible across Q3: Cymphony ($25M), Onyx Security ($113M), AIR Security ($40-50M), and others are all building governance, monitoring, or firewall layers around enterprise AI agents. Israeli startups are capturing an outsized share of this emerging category.

- **The cyber juggernaut continues.** Israeli cybersecurity startups raised at least $2.425B across 26 disclosed rounds in Q3 2026 alone, roughly half the global total for the quarter. This is a four-year high. The cluster is shifting from traditional network and cloud security toward AI-native categories: agent security, identity governance, and data loss prevention.

- **AgriTech shows signs of life.** BloomX's $13M round stands out in a quarter dominated by AI and cyber. The involvement of agricultural cooperatives as investors and pilot customers is a pattern worth watching: it signals real demand rather than speculative interest.

- **Acqui-hires and fast exits are accelerating.** Nebius paid an estimated $100-130M for Inferize, a 10-month-old, 17-person team. Enso, with only $23M raised, is already acquiring service businesses. The velocity from founding to exit or acquisition is compressing, particularly for teams with strong pedigrees (Granulate alumni, RapidAPI founders).

- **The small-round drought deepens.** According to the Israel Innovation Authority, funding rounds below $10M have fallen to a decade low, while rounds above $50M surged to $8.4B in the first nine months. The barbell is getting more extreme: capital concentrates in proven categories and repeat founders, while early-stage companies face a harder fundraising environment.

- **Protego Ventures closed a $125M debut fund** (September 29) exclusively for Israeli defense tech, the first and largest dedicated defense VC in Israel. XTEND, its first portfolio company, IPO'd on NYSE at $1.5B. This signals institutional capital entering Israeli defense tech at scale.

Multiple players raised $25M+ rounds within weeks of each other, which typically precedes a land-grab and then market consolidation within 12-18 months. For the Catalyst mandate, the window to invest at reasonable valuations in this category is narrowing.
