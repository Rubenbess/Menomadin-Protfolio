# Menomadin Weekly Deal Report

**Week of September 7 - September 14, 2026**

A week dominated by defense and cybersecurity announcements. Covenant emerged from two years of stealth with $250M+ and a cruise missile program. Cylake, founded by Palo Alto Networks creator Nir Zuk, closed $245M for a pre-product cybersecurity platform. AI agent security continued its category formation with Cymphony's Sequoia-led Series A. On the impact side, WINT Water Intelligence closed a $36M Series D, validating the AI-driven water conservation thesis. Two notable M&A exits rounded out the week: Scanovate to Prytek for $50M and Bonfy.AI to Kiteworks. Total confirmed capital deployed: approximately $640M across 6 funding rounds and 2 acquisitions.

---

## Section 1: Top Deals

**Covenant** | Stealth Emergence | $250M+ (across 3 rounds)
- What they do: Develops low-cost, high-volume cruise missile systems under the product name Anthem. Designed for mass production in the thousands, targeting defense procurement cost structures that make current missile inventories unsustainable.
- Investors: Andreessen Horowitz, Founders Fund, Lux Capital, 8VC, Aleph, Lightspeed Venture Partners
- Why it matters: Valued at $1.25B at latest round. The company operated in total secrecy for two years before revealing both its technology and accumulated capital on September 9. The investor lineup combines Silicon Valley's most aggressive deep-tech backers (a16z, Founders Fund, Lux) with Aleph, Israel's flagship early-stage fund.
- Signals: Israeli-American co-founder Michael Kaufman and co-founder Ofir Dayan. Major operations in Israel. Two-year stealth period with top-tier VCs staying silent is rare. Defense procurement cycles favor companies that can deliver at scale and low cost.
- Suggested angle: Outside Menomadin's direct mandate sectors, but the autonomous defense supply chain intersects with deeptech and AI. Track the ecosystem of sub-tier Israeli suppliers that Covenant's production ramp will create.

**Cylake** | Convertible Bond | $245M
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber]
- What they do: Building a complete cybersecurity platform designed for full data sovereignty, intended for on-premises and private environments that require operational control and accountability. No product in market yet; beta expected by end of 2026, full availability in 2027.
- Investors: Lightspeed Venture Partners, Picture Capital, Redpoint Ventures
- Why it matters: This is a pre-product raise of $245M, bringing total funding to $290M (following a $45M Greylock-led seed in March 2026). The bet is entirely on the founding team: Nir Zuk created Palo Alto Networks and served as its CTO, co-founder Ehud Shamir co-founded SentinelOne, and co-founder Wilson Xu rounds out the technical leadership. Cylake is building for a market that rejects cloud-delivered security, a contrarian position that could capture government, defense and regulated-industry budgets.
- Signals: Three top-tier VC firms funding a pre-beta cybersecurity company at this scale. Nir Zuk is one of the most consequential repeat founders in cybersecurity history. Palo Alto Networks is currently valued above $120B.
- Suggested angle: Too early-stage paradoxically for Series A co-investment (no product yet) but too well-capitalized to need it. Monitor the on-prem/sovereign security thesis Cylake represents. If the category takes hold, earlier-stage Israeli companies building sovereign-cloud or data-residency tooling will become attractive.

**Scanovate / Prytek** | M&A | $50M (first phase; up to $100M total)
[MANDATE FIT: Fintech]
- What they do: Digital identity verification and customer lifecycle management for financial institutions. Founded 2014 by Amir Fishman, Roy Fishman and Irad Gilboa. Bootstrapped company serving 250+ customers including ~120 banks. Subsidiaries Cellosign (process digitization) and ADO Technologies (LatAm biometric authentication) included in the deal.
- Investors: Prytek Group (acquirer)
- Why it matters: A bootstrapped Israeli fintech reaching $50-100M exit without ever raising VC capital. Scanovate will lead Prytek's customer lifecycle management segment. The deal validates the thesis that digital identity infrastructure for financial institutions is a durable, acquirable business.
- Signals: Bootstrapped exit is increasingly rare and notable. Prytek expanding its Israel footprint through acquisition. The digital identity and KYC space continues to consolidate.
- Suggested angle: The fintech identity verification space remains fragmented at the early stage. Israeli startups building regulatory-compliant identity infrastructure for banking could follow a similar bootstrapped-to-acquisition path.

**WINT Water Intelligence** | Series D | $36M
[MANDATE FIT: CleanTech/Energy/Water]
- What they do: AI-powered water leak detection and prevention platform for commercial buildings. Uses IoT sensors and machine learning to identify leaks in real time, automatically shutting off water flow to prevent damage. Platform saved 1.15 billion gallons of water and prevented an estimated $100 million in property damage in the past year.
- Investors: LIP Ventures, Inven Capital (co-leads)
- Why it matters: Nearly 600 enterprise customers with 30,000+ installed systems worldwide. Customer base grew 40%+ in 2025. The company has moved past proving the technology into scaling a global distribution model. Water conservation is becoming a regulatory and insurance requirement, not just a sustainability initiative.
- Signals: Inven Capital returning from Series C to co-lead Series D signals strong execution. Total funding now exceeds $70M. The intersection of water conservation, building insurance and AI creates a defensible market position.
- Suggested angle: Direct Impact mandate fit. While WINT is at Series D, it validates the water-tech thesis. Track earlier-stage Israeli water-tech companies that address complementary problems (water quality, agricultural water management, infrastructure monitoring) for Series A investment.

**Cymphony** | Series A | $25M ($30M total with seed)
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber]
- What they do: Enterprise security platform for AI agent identity management. Builds a "workforce graph" that maps employees, AI agents and non-human identities across corporate systems, giving security teams visibility into who and what has access. Valued at $100M+ post-investment.
- Investors: Sequoia Capital and SMBC Fin Atlas Beyond Fund (co-leads); Sequoia also led previously undisclosed seed
- Why it matters: Sequoia doubling down from seed to Series A in an Israeli cybersecurity startup is a high-conviction signal. The company was founded by Talpiot graduates Shy Dekel, Idan Berkovits and Edi Gotlieb, all with elite technical roles in Israeli defense and intelligence. AI agent identity is becoming the next battleground after last week's AIR Security raised $50M for a related but distinct problem (agent supply chain security vs. agent identity governance).
- Signals: Sequoia leading both rounds. Talpiot founding team. $100M+ valuation at Series A. The AI agent security category is fragmenting into sub-categories (supply chain, identity, governance) faster than most new security categories.
- Suggested angle: Series A at $25M with Sequoia leading is above Catalyst's typical check size, but the AI agent identity/governance space is rapidly forming. Look for seed-stage Israeli startups addressing adjacent problems: agent compliance, agent audit trails, agent access policy enforcement.

**Euno** | Series A | $23M
[MANDATE FIT: AI]
- What they do: Enterprise data platform that learns how organizations work and provides contextual intelligence to autonomous AI agents. The "context brain" enables agents to understand organizational processes, relationships and workflows rather than operating with generic instructions.
- Investors: N47 (lead), 10D, angels including Wiz co-founder Yinon Kostika, Cyera co-founder Yotam Segev, Eon co-founder Ofir Ehrlich, and Tavily founder Rotem Weiss
- Why it matters: As enterprises deploy more AI agents, the bottleneck is shifting from model capability to organizational context. Euno addresses the gap between what an agent can do and what it should do given a specific company's processes and data. Total funding now $29M.
- Signals: Angel roster reads like a directory of Israel's top enterprise/security founders (Wiz, Cyera, Eon, Tavily). This level of founder-angel backing at Series A suggests strong product validation within the Israeli enterprise-tech network.
- Suggested angle: Catalyst mandate fit (AI). $23M Series A is within range for a follow-on or co-investment conversation. The organizational-context layer for AI agents is a category that will scale with agent adoption. Worth a meeting.

**HydroSight** | Seed | $5M
[MANDATE FIT: CleanTech/Energy/Water, Deeptech]
- What they do: AI platform for mapping and analyzing the seabed. Integrates marine survey data from multiple sources and time periods to automatically identify changes, anomalies and patterns on the ocean floor. Serves ports, energy companies, telecom cable operators and maritime infrastructure operators.
- Investors: StageOne Ventures (lead), Vertex Ventures, HiCenter Ventures' National Center for the Blue Economy
- Why it matters: Founded in late 2025 in Haifa with five employees. Maritime infrastructure monitoring is a large, underserved market. The platform addresses a regulatory and operational need: underwater assets require regular inspection, and current methods are manual, expensive and error-prone.
- Signals: StageOne and Vertex Ventures leading together at seed. Blue economy is attracting dedicated capital (HiCenter's National Center for the Blue Economy). Maritime AI is a niche with limited competition.
- Suggested angle: Direct Impact mandate fit (CleanTech/Water). Seed stage with $5M raised places a Series A 12-18 months out. Check size ($0.5M-$5M) aligns with Impact mandate. Haifa-based, novel technology, underserved market. Recommend early outreach.

## Section 3: Market Insights

- **Cybersecurity repeat founders attract pre-product capital at unprecedented scale.** Cylake raised $290M total without a product in market. The bet is entirely on Nir Zuk's track record (Palo Alto Networks) and co-founder Ehud Shamir's (SentinelOne). This signals that investor appetite for proven Israeli cyber founders has decoupled from traditional milestone-based evaluation.

- **AI agent security is fragmenting into distinct sub-categories.** Last week saw AIR Security ($50M, agent supply chain). This week: Cymphony ($25M, agent identity/workforce graph) and Euno ($23M, agent context/organizational intelligence). The category is specializing before it consolidates, creating a window for early-stage funds to identify the sub-segments that will endure.

- **Defense tech went mainstream this week.** Covenant's stealth reveal ($250M+, a16z/Founders Fund) is the largest Israeli-connected defense-tech raise of 2026. Combined with last week's AITAN ($41M) and Easy Aerial ($20M), Israeli defense-tech is drawing capital from Silicon Valley's most prominent non-defense investors.

- **Water and maritime tech showing renewed investor interest.** WINT ($36M Series D) and HydroSight ($5M Seed) represent both growth-stage validation and early-stage entry points in the blue economy. Regulatory pressure on water conservation and maritime infrastructure monitoring is creating investable categories.

- **Two bootstrapped exits in one week.** Both Scanovate ($50M+) and Bonfy.AI reached acquisition without significant VC funding. Scanovate bootstrapped entirely for 10 years before a $50-100M exit. These outcomes challenge the assumption that Israeli enterprise companies require venture backing to reach meaningful scale.
