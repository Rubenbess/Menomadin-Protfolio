## SECTION 1: TOP DEALS

**Xsight Labs** | Growth | $300M
[MANDATE FIT: Deeptech]
- What they do: Fabless semiconductor company building programmable networking silicon for hyperscale, edge, and AI data center networks. Products include switches and DPUs designed for next-generation AI cluster interconnects, including Ultra Ethernet Consortium standards.
- Investors: Led by Fidelity Management & Research Company, with participation from new and existing investors. Previous round in 2021 valued the company at $500M.
- Why it matters: A 5.6x valuation jump from $500M to $2.8B reflects the explosive demand for custom networking silicon as AI training clusters scale. Xsight's chips are deployed on SpaceX's V3 satellites, validating performance at the extreme edge. The company is positioned at the intersection of two structural tailwinds: AI infrastructure buildout and the shift away from proprietary networking hardware.
- Signals: Founded in 2017, HQ in Tel Aviv with offices in Kiryat Gat, Haifa, Boston, Raleigh, and San Jose. Fidelity leading a $300M round at this stage signals public-market-grade conviction. Multi-generational roadmap suggests pipeline visibility with Tier-1 hyperscalers.
- Suggested angle: Well beyond Menomadin's check size, but this deal maps the competitive landscape for Israeli semiconductor and AI infrastructure companies. Track seed-stage Israeli startups building complementary silicon or networking software that could benefit from the same AI data center buildout.

**Onyx Security** | Series B | $113M
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber]
- What they do: AI agent security platform that discovers, monitors, and remediates risks created by autonomous AI agents operating across enterprise environments. Centerpiece product is the Guardian Agent, a supervisory layer that watches other agents in real time and intervenes when they take unauthorized actions.
- Investors: Led by Bessemer Venture Partners, with participation from Cyberstarts, TCV, and others. Total funding exceeds $153M.
- Why it matters: Revenue quadrupled in the four months since stealth exit. Anthropic announced integration with Onyx to secure enterprise AI adoption, a signal that foundation model providers see third-party agent governance as essential infrastructure. The speed from stealth to $640M valuation reflects how fast the AI agent security category is crystallizing.
- Signals: Founded by Unit 8200 veteran Maxim Bar Kogan and AI entrepreneur Gil Elbaz. 80+ employees across Israel, US, and Canada. Bessemer leading both the seed and Series B is rare double-down conviction.
- Suggested angle: Too late for direct entry, but Onyx's trajectory confirms AI agent security as the fastest-growing cyber sub-category. Look for Israeli seed-stage companies building adjacent capabilities: agent identity, policy orchestration, or agent-specific threat intelligence. These are the Series A opportunities within the next 12 months.

**groundcover** | Series C | $100M
[MANDATE FIT: B2B SaaS]
- What they do: Full-stack cloud observability platform that keeps operational data inside the customer's own cloud environment rather than shipping it to an external monitoring provider. Positions itself as an alternative to Datadog for the AI era, where data volumes are surging beyond what legacy SaaS monitoring can handle affordably.
- Investors: Led by One Peak, with participation from Morgan Stanley Expansion Capital and existing investors Zeev Ventures, Angular Ventures, Heavybit, and Jibe. Total raised: $160M.
- Why it matters: Revenue tripled and headcount doubled over the past year. 250+ paying customers. The thesis that AI workloads generate observability data at a scale that breaks traditional SaaS economics is playing out in real contracts. A $500M valuation for an Israeli B2B SaaS company at Series C with this growth profile signals a credible IPO trajectory.
- Signals: Founded by CEO Shahar Azulay and CTO Yechezkel Rabinovich. 140 employees, 80 in Tel Aviv. Morgan Stanley's participation at this stage often precedes public-market conversations.
- Suggested angle: Beyond Menomadin's entry point, but validates the thesis that AI-era infrastructure creates new B2B SaaS categories. Track Israeli startups building adjacent observability or FinOps tooling for AI workloads at earlier stages.

**Enigma** | Seed | $71M
[MANDATE FIT: AI, Deeptech]
- What they do: Develops foundation AI models for robots alongside software and interfaces designed to make robotic systems easier to deploy and operate. Launched the world's first interactive AI robotics experience where anyone can control real robots remotely in real time. Training approach reduces the need for manually collected physical data and works across different robots and environments.
- Investors: Led by Index Ventures and Ribbit Capital, with participation from Conviction Partners and angel investors from OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, and Cognition.
- Why it matters: $71M is one of the largest seed rounds globally in 2026. The investor roster reads like a who's who of frontier AI: angels from OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, and Cognition all backing a single robotics company. This signals that top AI researchers believe general-purpose robot interfaces, not just better models, are the bottleneck to physical AI adoption.
- Signals: Founded by CEO Jonathan Jacobi and CTO Gal Niv, both Unit 8200 veterans. Less than a year old. Index Ventures leading consecutive mega-rounds in Israeli AI (also led Frame, Glow investments) reflects concentrated thesis-driven deployment.
- Suggested angle: Seed-stage but far above Menomadin's check size range. The deal does signal that robotics middleware and interface layers are where smart capital sees the next platform opportunity. Watch for smaller Israeli startups building robot perception, simulation, or deployment tooling.

**Act Security** | Seed + Series A | $60M ($20M Seed + $40M Series A)
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber]
- What they do: Action-centric cloud security platform that eliminates exploit conditions by systematically reducing access surfaces across cloud infrastructure. Enforces boundaries for humans, workloads, and AI agents. Addresses the spiraling patch problem created by AI's ability to discover new vulnerabilities faster than teams can remediate them.
- Investors: Seed led by Team8 and Bessemer Venture Partners, with Hetz Ventures and Claltech. Series A led by Notable Capital, with Startpoint Capital and SVCI.

Raising $60M across seed and Series A before public launch, with both Team8 and Bessemer in the cap table, mirrors the playbook that produced several of Israel's largest cybersecurity exits. The premise that AI-discovered vulnerabilities require a fundamentally different remediation architecture is timely and defensible.
- Signals: Repeat founders with a $500M+ exit track record (Epsagon to Cisco, Medigate to Claroty). Team8 and Bessemer co-leading seed is a strong signal. Notable Capital leading the Series A adds enterprise distribution muscle.
- Suggested angle: Stage and check size are above Menomadin's typical entry, but the AI-native cloud security thesis aligns with Catalyst mandate. Track the broader cloud security refresh happening as agentic workloads force a rethink of access control models.

**Cyera acquires Oasis Security** | M&A | $1B
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber]
- What they do: Cyera (data security, $12B valuation) is acquiring Oasis Security (non-human identity management for AI agents) to build a unified platform for governing both data and machine identities across enterprise environments.
- Deal structure: Mostly cash with remainder in Cyera shares. Expected to close later in 2026.
- Why it matters: Second-largest Israeli cyber M&A of 2026 after Accenture/Dragos ($3.2B). Announced weeks after Cyera raised $600M at $12B. Confirms that the market for securing AI agent identities is consolidating before the category fully matures, with strategic acquirers moving aggressively to lock in technology. Oasis was founded in 2022 and reached $1B exit value in under four years.
- Signals: Rapid exit timeline for Oasis validates the non-human identity security thesis. Cyera's willingness to deploy $1B in acquisition capital signals confidence in enterprise demand for combined data + identity security.
- Suggested angle: This acquisition sets the competitive map for non-human identity security. Israeli startups building in adjacent areas (agent credential management, machine-to-machine auth, API security for AI agents) should be on Menomadin's radar as potential Series A targets.

## SECTION 3: MARKET INSIGHTS

- **Cybersecurity dominance continues.** Five of seven funding rounds this week were cybersecurity companies, and the sole M&A deal was also in cyber. AI agent security, endpoint protection, and cloud access management are the sub-categories absorbing the most capital.

- **Bessemer Venture Partners appeared in three deals this week** (Onyx Security Series B lead, Act Security Seed co-lead, and the broader Cyera ecosystem via prior investment), confirming them as the most active institutional investor in Israeli cyber right now.

- **AI agent governance is consolidating rapidly.** Onyx ($113M Series B), Act Security ($60M), and the Cyera/Oasis acquisition ($1B) all target the problem of controlling what AI agents can access and do inside enterprises. The category barely existed 12 months ago.

- **Seed rounds continue to inflate.** Enigma ($71M), Act Security ($60M combined), Harmony ($34M), and Bloom Security ($20M) are all at or above what would have been Series A or B rounds two years ago. Israeli founders with prior exits are commanding outsized seed commitments, compressing the traditional funding ladder.

- **Index Ventures is deploying aggressively in Israel.** After leading Glow ($180M), Frame ($50M), and now Enigma ($71M) in July alone, Index is running one of the most concentrated Israeli tech deployment strategies among global venture firms.

- **No Impact-sector deals this week.** All confirmed deals fell within Catalyst mandate sectors. HealthTech, Education, CleanTech, AgriTech, FoodTech, and Accessibility sectors were quiet during this period.

## SECTION 4: SPECIAL FLAGS

- **Repeat founders across multiple deals.** Act Security's team previously built Medigate (acquired by Claroty for $500M+). Harmony's founders previously built Epsagon (acquired by Cisco for $500M). Bloom Security was founded by Palo Alto Networks veterans. This concentration of repeat founders in a single week signals that experienced Israeli operators are re-entering the market with strong conviction in the AI security and enterprise AI thesis.

- **Competitive pressure in Catalyst sectors.** The volume of well-funded Israeli cybersecurity startups emerging from stealth (Glow, Neo, Onyx, Act, Bloom, Frame within a single month) creates a crowded landscape. For Menomadin's Catalyst mandate, this means differentiation will matter more than category entry. Prioritize companies with distinct technical moats or underserved sub-segments rather than broad-platform plays.

- **Cyera/Oasis acquisition signals exit velocity in non-human identity.** Oasis reached a $1B exit in under four years. Israeli seed-stage companies building in the non-human identity and machine credential space could follow a similar compressed timeline. This is a high-priority area for Series A prospecting within the Cyber mandate.

- **No high-priority outreach targets this week.** All funding rounds were at check sizes above Menomadin's range, and the companies that raised are already well-capitalized. However, the thematic signals (AI agent governance, AI-native endpoint security, robotic interfaces) point to where seed-stage opportunities will emerge in Q3-Q4 2026. Recommend proactive sourcing in these verticals.
