## Section 3: Market Insights

- **H1 2026 closes at record pace.** Reports published this week confirmed Israeli startups raised between $8.4B and $8.6B in H1 2026, up 45-52% year-over-year. This represents one of the strongest half-year performances in Israeli tech history, fueled by concentrated mega-rounds in AI and cybersecurity. However, the number of rounds fell roughly 35%, confirming the ongoing trend of more capital flowing into fewer companies.

- **The concentration paradox deepens.** Five deals alone (AppsFlyer $1.3B, Cyera $600M, DriveNets $410M, Decart $300M, Dream $260M) accounted for nearly $3B, or roughly one-third of total H1 capital. For a Series A fund, this means the best opportunities are at the seed and early stages before companies reach this winner-take-most dynamic. Serial founders now account for 39% of all rounds.

- **Cybersecurity investment more than doubled vs. H1 2025.** Identity security (NewCore), offensive AI (A Security), cloud prevention (Aryon), data governance (Cyera), browser security (LayerX exit), and sovereign AI (Dream) all raised significant capital in June alone. The theme is uniform: traditional security architectures are inadequate for environments populated by autonomous AI agents.

- **Exit market remains healthy.** The LayerX/Akamai completion at $205M joins a strong H1 2026 exit list that includes Cisco/Astrix ($400M), Motorola/D-Fend ($1.5B), Apple/Q.ai ($1.5B), and Nuvei/Payoneer ($2.75B). Strategic acquirers are aggressively buying Israeli AI and cybersecurity capabilities. More than 50 M&A transactions closed in H1 2026.

- **Fund formation signals continued LP appetite.** Deep33 closed its first deep-tech fund at $200M (above its $150M target), monday.com launched a $200M venture arm, and the Israeli government channeled $450M into venture capital through new VC fund programs. The infrastructure to sustain deal flow into 2027 is being built now.

- **Defense tech matured as a standalone asset class.** Traysar ($25M seed, tunnel warfare), D-Fend ($1.5B exit, counter-drone), Kela ($200M, military OS), and Anduril's exploration of Israeli operations all confirm defense technology has moved from niche to institutional.
