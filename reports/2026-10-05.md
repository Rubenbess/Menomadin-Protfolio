# Menomadin Investments Weekly Deal Report

**Period:** September 28 - October 5, 2026

## SECTION 1: TOP DEALS

Deal flow this week was lighter than the preceding weeks of September, as the Israeli tech ecosystem wound down for Sukkot (September 25 - October 2) and Simchat Torah (October 2-3). Most announcements clustered around September 29, just before the holiday break. The deals that did land reinforced the dominant theme of Q3 2026: securing and governing AI agents inside the enterprise.

---

**Reco** | Growth | $55M
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber]
- What they do: Reco builds an identity and permissions graph that maps how AI agents, applications, and employees interact across enterprise systems, giving security teams visibility into agent sprawl and the ability to restrict unauthorized access.
- Investors: AT&T Ventures (strategic, also a customer), Forestay, Quadrille Capital. Total funding now $140M.
- Why it matters: AT&T converting from customer to strategic investor at this scale signals that large enterprises are moving AI agent security from evaluation to a procurement priority. Reco is one of the few players with Fortune 500 production deployments.
- Signals: AT&T's involvement as both customer and investor is a strong validation signal. Rapid follow-on from the $30M Series B closed in February 2026. ~140 employees split between Israel and the US.
- Suggested angle: The round size and strategic backing put Reco beyond Menomadin's direct check range, but the deal confirms that AI agent security is no longer a niche bet. Track the ecosystem of smaller companies building adjacent tooling (identity, permissions, governance) in this space.

---

**HyperGuest** | Growth | $25M
[MANDATE FIT: B2B SaaS]
- What they do: HyperGuest provides infrastructure that connects hotels directly with booking platforms, travel agencies, and AI agents without requiring a separate technical integration for each partnership. The platform has processed over $1 billion in transactions.
- Investors: AMI (mid-market arm of Apax Partners). Previous investors include Lightspeed and Viola Ventures.
- Why it matters: The investment from Apax's mid-market arm signals a company transitioning from venture-scale to growth-equity territory. HyperGuest's pivot to positioning as the connectivity layer for AI-driven travel distribution is timely as AI agents begin handling booking workflows.
- Signals: 160 employees across 21 countries. $1B+ in transaction volume. The AI agent distribution angle is relatively new and differentiated.
- Suggested angle: Too late for a Series A entry, but worth tracking as a potential acquirer or partner for earlier-stage Israeli travel-tech companies building AI agent interfaces.

---

**enso** | Series A | $15M
[MANDATE FIT: AI, B2B SaaS]
- What they do: enso builds AI agent systems for marketing and sales, coined "Agentic Growth Hacking." The agents analyze how platforms rank, recommend, and cite content, then run small-scale experiments and turn effective ones into repeatable growth mechanisms.
- Investors: MoreTech Ventures (lead), NFX, Phenomen VC, Gil Hirsch. Total funding: $23M.
- Why it matters: Founded by Mickey Haslavsky (co-founder of RapidAPI), enso targets a practical pain point: how companies maintain visibility and growth in an AI-mediated discovery environment. The go-to-market model (embedding an engineer and marketer per customer) is capital-intensive but drives high retention.
- Signals: Repeat founder with a significant exit. Customers include Papaya Global, MyHeritage, Natural Intelligence, and Winn AI. 26 employees, mostly Israel-based.
- Suggested angle: At $15M Series A, enso sits within Catalyst check range. The repeat founder, strong early customer base, and the shift in how AI is reshaping content discovery make this worth a closer look. The embedded delivery model is a double-edged sword: high NRR but harder to scale.

**Rig Security** | Seed | $12M
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber]
- What they do: Rig Security builds a platform that identifies AI agents operating through human and machine accounts, distinguishing between people and AI to stop risky agent activity without blocking employees. Uses graph-based security analysis to map relationships between agents, identities, and resources.
- Investors: Ten Eleven Ventures and Brightmind Partners (co-leads), CrowdStrike Falcon Fund, Wiz co-founder Ami Luttwak. Total: $12M.
- Why it matters: The identity layer is becoming the critical chokepoint as enterprises deploy AI agents that inherit human credentials. Rig's 96% accuracy claim in identity correlation and existing Fortune 200 deployments are strong for a seed-stage company.
- Signals: Tel Aviv-based. Wiz co-founder backing. CrowdStrike strategic interest. Already operational in Fortune 200 enterprises processing hundreds of millions of identity events monthly.
- Suggested angle: Strong Catalyst fit at seed stage. The $12M seed with tier-1 cyber investors and a Wiz co-founder as backer positions Rig well for a rapid Series A. This is worth an immediate outreach to understand whether co-investment or follow-on participation is possible.

**Inferize (acquired by Nebius)** | M&A | $100-150M
[MANDATE FIT: AI, Deeptech]
- What they do: Inferize developed technology to optimize GPU utilization by eliminating cold starts during AI model launches, dynamically aligning resource usage with actual demand to reduce idle computational capacity.
- Acquirer: Nebius Group (NASDAQ: NBIS), which folded Inferize into its Token Factory managed AI inference platform.
- Why it matters: A 10-month-old Israeli startup achieving a $100-150M exit to a public company validates the "rapid AI infrastructure exit" thesis. Nebius has now acquired two Israeli AI startups in 2026 (Tavily for up to $400M in February and Inferize in October), establishing Israel as a go-to source for AI infrastructure talent.
- Signals: Founded January 2026, acquired October 2026. Team composed of AI researchers from Google/DeepMind and Unit 8200 veterans. Nebius building an AI development center in Israel.
- Suggested angle: The speed of this exit underscores the premium on AI infrastructure talent. Watch for other pre-seed and seed-stage Israeli teams building GPU optimization, inference efficiency, and model serving infrastructure. Nebius is now an active acquirer to track.

**Protego Ventures** | VC Fund Close | $125M
- What they do: Israel's first and largest dedicated defense tech venture capital fund, investing $5-15M per company in startups addressing defense and security needs.
- Led by: Lital Leshem and Lee Moser. Backed by executives from Ares Management.
- Why it matters: The fund validates dedicated defense-tech investing in Israel as a standalone category. First portfolio company XTEND went public on NYSE in September at a $1.5B valuation, potentially becoming a "fund maker."

- Suggested angle: Protego's $5-15M check range overlaps with Menomadin's Catalyst mandate. Track their portfolio for co-investment opportunities in dual-use (defense + commercial) AI and autonomy companies.

## SECTION 3: MARKET INSIGHTS

- **Sukkot holiday dampened deal volume.** The Sep 28 - Oct 5 window overlapped almost entirely with Sukkot and Simchat Torah, explaining why announced deal count was significantly lower than the preceding weeks of September, which saw $2.77B raised across 28 rounds. Expect a catch-up burst in the second and third weeks of October.

- **AI agent security is consolidating into a defined category.** Three of the five deals this week (Reco, Rig Security, and the broader context from Cymphony and AIR earlier in September) target the same thesis: enterprises need to understand, govern, and secure AI agents operating alongside employees. The market is segmenting into identity/access (Rig, Cymphony), traffic/firewall (Huskeys, AIR), and governance/visibility (Reco). At the current pace, expect M&A consolidation in this space within 12-18 months.

- **Nebius is the most active acquirer of Israeli AI infrastructure.** Two acquisitions in 2026 (Tavily for up to $400M and Inferize for $100-150M), plus plans for an AI data center in Modi'in, position Nebius as a strategic buyer to monitor. Their appetite for very early-stage companies (both targets were under 18 months old) creates a new exit path for Israeli AI infrastructure startups.

- **September 2026 in context: $2.77B across 28 rounds, dominated by five mega-deals.** Wonderful ($550M), Island ($400M), Cyera ($400M), Upwind ($300M), and Covenant ($250M) accounted for $1.9B or 69% of all capital. The remaining 23 rounds averaged $38M, indicating healthy mid-market activity beneath the headline numbers.

- **Defense tech is maturing as a standalone investment category in Israel.** Protego's $125M fund close, XTEND's NYSE IPO at $1.5B, Covenant's $250M emergence from stealth, and ASIO's $15M round all point to defense tech graduating from a subsector of cybersecurity into its own category, with dedicated capital, talent pipelines, and exit paths.

## SECTION 4: SPECIAL FLAGS

- **Repeat founder: Mickey Haslavsky (enso).** Co-founder of RapidAPI. Now building enso, an AI growth platform. Series A at $15M with MoreTech Ventures. Falls within Catalyst mandate (AI, B2B SaaS). Worth an immediate introduction.

- **Competitive signal: AI agent security crowding.** At least six Israeli startups have raised meaningful rounds in AI agent security in Q3 2026 alone (Reco, Rig Security, Cymphony, AIR, Huskeys, and elements of Island's expansion). This creates both opportunity (the category is real) and risk (commoditization pressure on any single player). For Menomadin's Catalyst mandate, the seed-stage entry points are narrowing fast. Rig Security's $12M seed may be one of the last accessible entry points at this stage.

- **High-priority opportunity: Rig Security.** $12M seed, Tel Aviv-based, Wiz co-founder backing, CrowdStrike strategic interest, Fortune 200 production deployments at seed stage. The combination of pedigree, traction, and strategic investor interest at seed stage is rare. Recommend immediate outreach this week before the post-holiday deal cycle picks up.

- **Ecosystem signal: Nebius building Israel AI hub.** Nebius's two acquisitions plus data center plans create a new magnet for AI infrastructure talent in Israel. This could spawn a wave of small Israeli teams building AI infrastructure tools specifically designed to be acquisition targets for cloud and infrastructure platforms.
