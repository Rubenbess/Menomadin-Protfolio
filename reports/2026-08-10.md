# Menomadin Investments Weekly Deal Report

**Week of August 3 - 10, 2026**

## Section 1: Top Deals

**Visa / BioCatch** | M&A Exit | $2.4B
- What they do: BioCatch analyzes behavioral biometrics during digital banking sessions, using keystroke patterns, touch gestures, and device handling to distinguish legitimate users from fraudsters in real time. Protects 1.8 billion devices across 350+ banking clients in 21 countries.
- Acquirer: Visa (from Permira and other shareholders)
- Why it matters: This is one of the largest Israeli tech exits on record and validates behavioral biometrics as a category worth billions. Visa is embedding fraud prevention deeper into its network rather than treating it as a point solution. Account takeovers and scams now cost over $1 trillion annually.
- Signals: Strategic acquisition by a payments infrastructure giant signals fraud detection is becoming a platform-level capability, not a standalone product. Permira exits with a strong return.
- Suggested angle: Track startups building complementary fraud prevention layers, particularly those focused on AI-generated identity fraud and real-time transaction monitoring where BioCatch leaves gaps.

**Zenity** | Series C | $125M
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber, AI]
- What they do: Tel Aviv-based platform that secures autonomous AI agents in production by understanding agent intent and deterministically allowing, modifying, or blocking their actions. Distinguishes between legitimate work and manipulated, compromised, or rogue behavior.
- Investors: Norwest (lead), SoftBank Vision Fund 2, Qumra Capital, Hitachi Ventures, LG Technology Ventures, Vertex Ventures, Third Point Ventures, DTCP, Intel Capital
- Why it matters: AI agent security is emerging as a distinct category. With frontier models escaping test sandboxes and agentic browsers being manipulated, the attack surface is expanding faster than traditional security can adapt. Total raised now $180M+.
- Signals: SoftBank, Hitachi, and LG joining signals enterprise demand from Asia-Pacific. Norwest leading at Series C indicates strong revenue trajectory.
- Suggested angle: Agent security will become table stakes. Track adjacent plays in agent observability, policy enforcement, and supply chain security for AI pipelines. Zenity's success validates the category for earlier-stage competitors.

**Oligo Security** | Growth Round | $60M
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber]
- What they do: Runtime security platform providing continuous visibility into code execution and real-time protection for applications, cloud workloads, and AI systems. AWS Security Hub's exclusive AI runtime security partner.
- Investors: Ballistic Ventures, Canon Capital, Greenfield Partners, Lightspeed Venture Partners, Red Dot Capital Partners, TLV Partners; angel Eyal Waldman (Mellanox co-founder)
- Why it matters: ARR grew 300% year-over-year, valuation more than doubled since Series B. The shift toward runtime security reflects a market realizing that pre-deployment scanning is insufficient when AI accelerates exploit development. Total raised: $140M.
- Signals: Eyal Waldman's involvement as an angel is notable given his track record. AWS partnership provides significant distribution.
- Suggested angle: Runtime security is becoming a core infrastructure layer. Monitor how AI-driven exploit development reshapes the defensive playbook, creating opportunities for companies that can act in real time.

**QuantHealth** | Series B | $45M
[MANDATE FIT: HealthTech]
- What they do: AI-driven clinical trial simulation platform that enables pharma companies to test and optimize trial designs through simulation before patient enrollment. Partners with 12 of the top 20 pharma companies globally.
- Investors: Qumra Capital (lead), Pitango HealthTech, Sanofi Ventures, Artofin Venture Capital, Bertelsmann Healthcare Investments, GC Ventures, NewHealth Ventures, Shoni Top Ventures, Esplanade Ventures
- Why it matters: Clinical trial failure rates remain above 90% in early phases. Simulation-first development has the potential to cut billions in wasted spend across the industry. Sanofi investing as both a strategic partner and customer is a strong validation signal. Total raised: ~$70M.
- Signals: The presence of both strategic (Sanofi) and financial investors suggests the company is crossing from early-stage experimentation into enterprise-wide deployment.
- Suggested angle: High-priority opportunity for Impact mandate. HealthTech companies enabling faster, cheaper drug development align with the thesis. Consider direct outreach.

**Deel / Clarity** | M&A Exit | ~$45-50M
- What they do: Clarity developed continuous identity verification and deepfake detection technology, specializing in detecting AI-generated identities during hiring and workforce management. Founded by Unit 8200 alumni.
- Acquirer: Deel ($45-50M in cash and shares)
- Why it matters: One in four candidate profiles will be fraudulent by 2028 according to Gartner. Deel embeds identity verification into global HR infrastructure, making deepfake defense a platform feature rather than a point tool. This is Deel's 15th acquisition, announced alongside hitting $1.5B in ARR.
- Signals: The acquisition validates deepfake detection as a horizontal need beyond cybersecurity. Deel's pace of acquisition signals aggressive platform consolidation.
- Suggested angle: Identity verification and deepfake defense are underfunded relative to the threat. Track startups applying these capabilities beyond HR to financial services, healthcare, and government.

**Mate Security** | Series A | $35M
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber, AI]
- What they do: AI-driven security operations platform that uses specialized AI agents to autonomously triage and respond to security incidents. Context-first architecture built on a deep-learning threat model.
- Investors: Canaan Partners (lead), Microsoft M12, Insight Partners, Team8
- Why it matters: Founders are ex-Wiz, Axonius, and Microsoft threat intelligence leaders. The company has grown 500%+ since Q3 2025, just eight months after emerging from stealth with a $15.5M Seed. Total raised now exceeds $50M.
- Signals: Microsoft M12 investing alongside Canaan and Insight is a strong signal of enterprise readiness. The Wiz alumni pedigree opens doors and attracts talent.
- Suggested angle: Agentic SOC automation is a rapidly consolidating category. Mate's trajectory makes it worth monitoring for competitive dynamics against incumbent SOAR vendors.

**Bloom Security** | Seed | $20M
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber]
- What they do: AI-native endpoint security platform that inventories AI agents, MCP servers, browser extensions, and code packages on employee endpoints, scoring risk by context rather than applying blanket blocks. Founded by Palo Alto Networks/Dig Security alumni.
- Investors: Glilot Capital Partners and Ten Eleven Ventures (co-leads), Okta Ventures, Runtime Ventures; angels from Snyk, Demisto, Dig Security, and Talon founders
- Why it matters: The endpoint attack surface is expanding beyond traditional malware to include AI agents, plugins, and extensions running on employee machines. This is a net-new category that legacy EDR was not built for. Already live at dozens of large enterprises in the US and Europe.
- Signals: $20M seed is large, indicating strong investor conviction. The angel roster reads like an endpoint security hall of fame.
- Suggested angle: Early-stage deal in a category with clear tailwinds. The $20M seed size puts it outside typical pre-seed check range, but the company and category are worth tracking closely.

## Section 3: Market Insights

- **Cybersecurity dominated the week.** Five of the nine confirmed deals were cybersecurity companies (Zenity, Oligo, Mate, Bloom, Above Security), spanning AI agent security, runtime protection, endpoint visibility, SOC automation, and insider threats. The sector continues to command the largest checks.

- **AI agent security is crystallizing as a distinct category.** Zenity's $125M round and Bloom's $20M seed both target the security implications of autonomous AI agents operating on enterprise infrastructure. With frontier models escaping sandboxes and agentic browsers being exploited, this sub-sector is attracting serious capital and talent.

- **Qumra Capital appeared twice this week**, leading QuantHealth's $45M Series B and participating in Zenity's $125M Series C. The firm is doubling down across AI-security and AI-healthcare, two of the highest-conviction verticals.

- **The exit market is alive.** Visa's $2.4B BioCatch acquisition and Deel's ~$50M Clarity pickup both closed in the same week, signaling that buyers are paying premium prices for trust, fraud detection, and identity verification capabilities embedded in platform infrastructure.

- **Founder pedigree drives outsized seed rounds.** Both Bloom ($20M) and Mate ($35M Series A, eight months after stealth) were founded by alumni of Palo Alto Networks, Wiz, Axonius, and Microsoft. Investors are underwriting teams with deep domain expertise and prior exits at historically large early-stage check sizes.

- **HealthTech continues to attract crossover capital.** QuantHealth's round included Sanofi Ventures as both investor and customer, reflecting the broader trend of pharma companies moving from arm's-length partnerships to direct equity stakes in their AI infrastructure providers.
