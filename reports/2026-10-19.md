## Section 1: Top Deals

**Varonis / Proofpoint** | M&A | $6.3 Billion
- What they do: Varonis is a data security and analytics platform that protects enterprise data across cloud and on-prem environments. Israeli-founded, Nasdaq-listed.
- Acquirer: Proofpoint (backed by Thoma Bravo). All-cash deal at $49.75/share, a 52% premium to pre-rumor price.
- Why it matters: This is the largest Israeli-connected cybersecurity exit of 2026 and creates one of the world's biggest pure-play cyber companies. Thoma Bravo is consolidating the data security stack.
- Signals: Bloomberg reported PE interest in June; Blackstone, Thoma Bravo, and Vista all circled. The final price represents 53x LTM FCF and 8.4x LTM revenue.
- Suggested angle: Track Varonis competitors and adjacent data security startups that may benefit from reduced public-market competition or see increased buyer interest.

**Axionic** | Pre-Seed (ongoing) | Up to $80M ($50M+ already committed)
[MANDATE FIT: Deeptech]
- What they do: Herzliya-based chip startup building inference-stage AI processors. Founded June 2026 by former Qualcomm executive Assaf Touboul. About 20 employees, half with PhDs.
- Investors: Amplify Partners, Wing VC, and Qualcomm Ventures co-leading, with Pitango, Viola, and 10D.
- Why it matters: An $80M pre-seed is extraordinary by any standard. The investor syndicate spans US and Israeli tier-1 funds, signaling high conviction in the team and market timing for dedicated AI inference silicon.
- Signals: Touboul's Qualcomm pedigree and the speed of capital commitment before product suggest deep investor relationships and a talent-driven bet.
- Suggested angle: Catalyst mandate. Deeptech, AI infrastructure. The round is still open and the investor base is strong. Worth tracking for potential co-investment at next stage.

**Zenzap** | Stealth Emergence | $44M Total ($33M Series A + $11M Seed)
[MANDATE FIT: AI, B2B SaaS]
- What they do: AI-native workplace messaging platform with built-in AI agents for workflow automation. Customers have created over 40,000 automated workflows. 45 employees across Tel Aviv, US, and Europe.
- Investors: Bessemer Venture Partners and Lightspeed Venture Partners co-led the Series A (closed August 2025). The Garage also participated.
- Why it matters: The stealth-to-public transition with $44M already raised and Bessemer/Lightspeed backing signals confidence in the enterprise AI-messaging category. The timing targets the gap between AI capability and workplace adoption.
- Signals: CEO Guy Weiss chose to stay in stealth through significant traction before going public. The BVP/Lightspeed combination is a strong signal for enterprise SaaS.
- Suggested angle: Catalyst mandate. B2B SaaS / AI. Already Series A, but worth tracking for Series B dynamics and competitive positioning against Slack/Teams AI features.

**Pluto Security** | Series A | $34M ($42M total)
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber]
- What they do: Platform that secures AI tools, employee-built applications, and browser extensions in enterprise environments. Targets "shadow AI" risk. Over 30 enterprise customers including Fortune 500 companies, with ARR above $2M.
- Investors: Jump Capital led the Series A. Skinos Ventures, AlphaDrive Ventures, and angels participated. TLV Partners led the earlier $8.5M seed.
- Why it matters: Shadow AI is an emerging enterprise pain point as employees adopt AI tools faster than IT can govern them. Pluto addresses a gap between cybersecurity and IT governance.
- Signals: Going from founding (2025) to $2M+ ARR and Fortune 500 customers in roughly a year is strong velocity. Three co-founders with security backgrounds.
- Suggested angle: Catalyst mandate. Cyber. Series A just closed, so Series B timing may align with Menomadin's check size. The shadow AI security category is heating up alongside Rein Security (see below).

**Geniez AI / Rocket Software** | M&A | ~$100M (estimated)
- What they do: AI agents that run on mainframes, monitoring live systems and diagnosing incidents. Only 6 employees. Raised $6M seed in September 2025 from StageOne Ventures and Canapi Ventures.
- Acquirer: Rocket Software (Waltham, MA). Technology feeds into Rocket EVA, their agentic AI platform.
- Why it matters: A 6-person team acquired for ~$100M roughly one year after its seed round is a remarkable return. It validates the thesis that AI agents for legacy infrastructure can command premium valuations.
- Signals: Repeat founders. CEO Gil Peleg and co-founder Dan Shprung previously built Model9, acquired by BMC Software in 2023. Same sector, same buyer profile.
- Suggested angle: Watch for more mainframe/legacy-AI acquisitions. The repeat-founder signal and rapid exit path are instructive for deal sourcing.

**Undecimal Bio** | Seed | $18M (oversubscribed)
[MANDATE FIT: HealthTech]
- What they do: Spun out from Eleven Therapeutics. Developing DELiveri, an AI-powered platform to deliver siRNA drugs beyond the liver. Operations in Cambridge and Tel Aviv. Targeting renovascular diseases initially.
- Investors: 4BIO Capital and IQ Capital co-led. Verve Ventures, Harel Tech, Entree Capital, Kerna Ventures, and Saras Capital participated.
- Why it matters: The "liver trap" is a well-known bottleneck in RNA therapeutics. If Undecimal can crack extra-hepatic delivery, it opens a large therapeutic space. The oversubscribed round at seed stage signals strong biotech investor interest.
- Signals: Yaniv Erlich (co-founder) is a recognized figure in computational genomics. The Eleven Therapeutics lineage gives the platform a head start.
- Suggested angle: Impact mandate. HealthTech. Seed stage, within Impact check-size range. RNA therapeutics delivery is a high-value area with clear unmet need.

**BloomX** | Series A | $13M
[MANDATE FIT: AgriTech]
- What they do: Robotic pollination systems that work alongside bees to improve crop yields. Growers report ~20% yield gains. Total funding now $20M.
- Investors: Elah Fund led. Rothschild Fund, OurCrowd, Sadot Innovation, and the Israel Innovation Authority participated.
- Why it matters: Pollinator decline is a systemic agricultural risk. BloomX offers a technology hedge with measurable yield impact across avocados, blueberries, and other crops. US and Latin American expansion underway.
- Signals: IIA participation validates the technology thesis. The investor mix (agri-focused fund plus generalist VCs) suggests broadening appeal.
- Suggested angle: Impact mandate. AgriTech. Series A, fits check-size range. Strong alignment with climate/sustainability themes and growing food-security concerns.

**Uvi** | Seed | $7.5M
[MANDATE FIT: AI, B2B SaaS]
- What they do: Connects AI agents to internal company data across Slack, Jira, Confluence, and code repositories. CEO Gilad Salinger says focus has shifted from accuracy to cost, tokens, and speed optimization.
- Investors: CT Innovations and Cardumen co-led. Mana VC, 91 Ventures, and Gaingles VC participated.
- Why it matters: As enterprises deploy AI agents at scale, the infrastructure to connect those agents to internal knowledge becomes a critical middleware layer. Uvi targets this plumbing.
- Signals: Dealroom ranks this round in the top 10% of Israeli seed deals. The problem statement (cost and speed of AI agent operations) is timely.
- Suggested angle: Catalyst mandate. AI / B2B SaaS. Early seed, within Impact check-size range. Track for Series A opportunity if traction develops.

**Loops / Salesforce** | M&A | ~$30M (estimated)
[MANDATE FIT: AI, B2B SaaS]
- What they do: AI-powered analytics platform that identifies drivers behind changing business metrics and recommends next steps. Founded 2020 by Tom Laufer and Ido Adiv. Raised $14M seed in 2022 from Scale Venture Partners.
- Acquirer: Salesforce. Technology will be integrated into Tableau.
- Why it matters: Salesforce's tenth Israeli acquisition. Validates the category of AI-driven business analytics that goes beyond dashboards to prescriptive insights.
- Signals: The Tableau integration path means the technology will reach Salesforce's massive install base. Modest exit for investors but strategic value for the product.
- Suggested angle: Track competing startups in AI business analytics that may attract similar acquisition interest from other platform companies.

## Section 3: Market Insights

- **Cybersecurity dominance continues.** The Varonis/Proofpoint deal ($6.3B) headlines a week where cyber accounted for both the largest exit and the largest new funding round (Pluto, $34M). Israeli cyber took nearly half of global sector funding in Q3 2026 according to CTech, and the M&A appetite from PE-backed acquirers (Thoma Bravo) shows no sign of slowing.

- **AI agent security is emerging as its own category.** Pluto Security ($34M) and Rein Security ($25M the prior week) both target the security of AI agents in enterprise environments but from different angles: Pluto focuses on employee-built AI tools and shadow AI, while Rein secures agents at runtime. Two funded companies in the same niche within one week suggests VCs see this as a distinct, investable category.

- **Repeat founders driving fast exits.** Geniez AI's founders (ex-Model9/BMC) achieved a ~$100M exit with 6 employees roughly one year after seed. Inferize's founders (ex-Granulate/Intel) reached $100-150M in under 10 months. The pattern: experienced founders with deep domain knowledge building focused products that larger platforms acquire for talent and technology.

- **Deeptech funding at unprecedented scale.** Axionic's $80M pre-seed for AI inference chips is extraordinary for the stage. Combined with the Amazon/Decart talks ($7B) and Nebius's continued acquisition spree, the market is putting significant capital into AI infrastructure companies with Israeli roots.

- **Israeli exits accelerating.** This week alone saw three confirmed exits (Varonis, Geniez AI, Loops) totaling over $6.4B, plus the reported Decart talks at $7B. Israeli startup M&A activity in 2026 is tracking well ahead of prior years. Salesforce's Loops acquisition marks its tenth Israeli deal, underscoring the country's role as an acqui-hire pipeline for major platforms.

- **Life sciences and agtech attracting specialized capital.** Undecimal Bio ($18M for RNA delivery) and BloomX ($13M for robotic pollination) both raised from sector-specialist investors (4BIO Capital, Elah Fund), indicating that deep-domain Israeli startups continue to find dedicated capital pools even as generalist VC attention concentrates on AI.
