## SECTION 1: TOP DEALS

**Glow** | Seed | $180M
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber]
- What they do: AI-native endpoint security platform designed to monitor and control autonomous AI agents, coding copilots, and automated workflows at the device level. Rebuilds endpoint protection from scratch for a post-CrowdStrike architecture.
- Investors: Led by Sequoia, Cyberstarts, Greenoaks, and Redpoint Ventures. Participation from Index Ventures, Swish Ventures, Lux Capital, Operator Collective, and Holly Ventures.
- Why it matters: A $1.2 billion valuation at stealth exit is the clearest signal yet that investors view agentic endpoint security as a distinct category from traditional EDR. The thesis is that CrowdStrike-era tools were built for human-driven endpoints; Glow is built for machine-driven ones.
- Signals: CEO Roi Tiger spent nine years at Meta as VP of Engineering. COO Emily Heath served as CISO at United Airlines and DocuSign and sat on Wiz's board through its $32B Google acquisition. Sequoia and Cyberstarts co-leading is a rare alignment of generalist and specialist conviction.
- Suggested angle: Stage is beyond Menomadin's typical check size, but this deal defines the competitive landscape for the agentic security category. Track the companies building adjacent tooling (agent observability, agent identity) that Glow will need to integrate with.

**Neo** | Series A | $100M ($75M Series A + $25M Seed)
[MANDATE FIT: Cyber]
- What they do: Enterprise platform for governing autonomous AI agents. Inventories AI agents, AI-powered applications, browser extensions, and digital identities while monitoring what those systems can access and how they behave. Positions itself as a new cybersecurity category: AI agent governance.
- Investors: Series A co-led by Andreessen Horowitz and Bessemer Venture Partners. Participation from Craft Ventures and Merlin Ventures. Seed round completed in 2025.
- Why it matters: The founding team represents a convergence of Israel's top cybersecurity companies. The $100M total raise in under a year, before any public product announcement, reflects deep conviction in the AI governance problem from two of the most active enterprise security investors.
- Signals: Founders are alumni of SentinelOne (CEO Nicholas Warner), Wiz, and Palo Alto Networks (CTO Eran Shirazi, CPO Shlomi Salem). HQ in Boston with R&D center in Tel Aviv. a16z and Bessemer co-leading is a statement-level vote of confidence.
- Suggested angle: Stage is above typical entry point, but the AI agent governance category is forming in real time. Watch for seed-stage Israeli companies building complementary tooling: agent audit trails, policy engines, runtime enforcement. This is where the Series A opportunities will emerge in 12-18 months.

**Elio** | Series A | $21M
[MANDATE FIT: AI]
- What they do: Develops AI-directed sensing hardware that integrates computation directly into the optical system. Before light reaches the sensor, dynamic layers of micromirrors process the information, functioning more like a neural network than a traditional lens. The AI system decides in real time what information it needs to capture.
- Investors: Led by Innovation Endeavors and Xora. Participation from Kevin Weil, Scribble VC, UpWest, and Resolute Ventures. Total funding to date: $29M.
- Why it matters: This is a deep-tech hardware play in a market dominated by software AI companies. The technology has applications across microscopy, semiconductor manufacturing, robotics, and defense. A genuinely novel approach to sensing that could become a platform technology.
- Signals: Co-founders Nadav Grossinger (CEO) and Nitay Romano (CTO) previously co-developed Pebbles Interfaces (acquired by Meta), then spent seven years leading physical sensing development for Meta's AR/VR headsets. Innovation Endeavors (Eric Schmidt's fund) leading signals credibility in deep tech.
- Suggested angle: Direct AI mandate fit at Series A. The dual-use nature (civilian and defense applications) broadens the exit surface. If the technology proves out in semiconductor inspection or drone detection, this could be a high-value platform. Worth a conversation with the founders to understand commercialization timeline and next-round dynamics.

**ASIO Technologies** | Seed | $15M
- What they do: Develops tactical systems for military forces operating in GPS-denied and communications-disrupted environments. Built on proprietary GeoFusion technology that enables navigation and targeting without satellite dependence. Systems are deployed across the IDF and with defense customers worldwide, including the US.
- Investors: Led by Protego Ventures with Aliya Capital Partners as co-lead.
- Why it matters: The round coincided with a contract win from Anduril worth tens of millions, validating product-market fit with a top-tier US defense buyer. GPS-independent navigation is an urgent operational requirement driven by electronic warfare proliferation.
- Signals: Founded by brothers Tomer and Yaniv Malchi. Combat-proven technology already in IDF use. Anduril partnership signals integration into the US defense industrial base.
- Suggested angle: Outside core mandate sectors. However, defense-tech continues to attract outsized capital in Israel. Track for dual-use applications that could spill into civilian navigation or autonomous systems.

**Plantopia** | Seed | $9M
[MANDATE FIT: AgriTech]
- What they do: Produces dairy proteins (starting with casein) through molecular farming, using indoor vertical farming of plant sprouts rather than fermentation or animal agriculture. The technology was developed at the Hebrew University's Faculty of Agriculture.
- Investors: Led by Schreiber Foods (US dairy giant). Participation from Siddhi Capital, Yotvata, Arkin Capital, and Fresh Start incubator. Total funding to date: $16M.
- Why it matters: Schreiber Foods leading is a strong strategic validation signal. Unlike fermentation-based competitors, Plantopia's sprout-based approach requires no special conditions, produces oxygen, and scales with standard vertical farming infrastructure. The manufacturing facility at Kibbutz Sdot Yam is expected to begin operations in Q3 2026.
- Signals: University spin-off with defensible IP. Strategic lead investor from the dairy industry (Schreiber is a $7B+ revenue company). Near-term path to commercial production.
- Suggested angle: Strong AgriTech mandate fit. Series A likely in 12-18 months once manufacturing facility is operational and first commercial product ships. This is the right stage and sector for Menomadin. Recommend outreach to founders this week.

**Cordant** | Seed | $8M
[MANDATE FIT: Fintech]
- What they do: Building a monitoring and coordination layer for financial institutions. The platform integrates with existing banking and payment systems to unify operational data and enable real-time decision-making across complex payment flows.
- Investors: Co-led by Motive Partners and Oak HC/FT. Participation from Bankless VC, FJ Labs, SignalFire, Quona, Next Stage, Selah Ventures, Flatironx, Nascent Ventures, Silvercircle Ventures, and Generative Ventures. Design partners include Bitso and Paxos.
- Why it matters: Payment infrastructure complexity is growing faster than the tools to manage it. Founded by four former executives of Israeli fintech unicorn Rapyd, who experienced the problem firsthand while building a global payments platform.
- Signals: Repeat founding team from Rapyd (Eric Rosenthal, Lior Levitt, Sagi Ittah, Juan Jose Huezo). Motive Partners, the leading fintech-specialist PE/VC firm, co-leading at seed is a strong category endorsement. Paxos and Bitso as design partners suggest real enterprise demand.
- Suggested angle: Fintech mandate fit. Seed stage, former unicorn founders, specialist investors. This profile matches Menomadin's check size and stage preference. Track for Series A timing and growth metrics.

**Zafran Security** | Strategic Investment | Undisclosed

- Investors: Strategic investment from Cisco Investments via SAFE agreement. Existing investors include Sequoia Capital, Cyberstarts, Menlo Ventures, PSP Growth, Vintage Investment Partners, American Express Ventures. Total funding past $140M.

## SECTION 3: MARKET INSIGHTS

- **Cybersecurity dominates the week.** Four of seven deals are cybersecurity companies. The combined disclosed capital flowing into Israeli cyber this week exceeds $290M. The sector is now clearly the highest-volume category in Israeli venture for 2026.

- **AI agent security is crystallizing as a category.** Glow (endpoint security for AI agents), Neo (AI agent governance), and Zafran (exposure management) all address different facets of the same problem: enterprises deploying autonomous AI systems without adequate security infrastructure. Three separate companies, three separate investor syndicates, all converging on the same thesis within days.

- **Cyberstarts appears in multiple rounds.** The Israeli cybersecurity-specialist fund participated in both Glow ($180M) and has existing positions in Zafran and multiple other Israeli cyber companies that raised this month. Their portfolio concentration signals a strong view on where the category is heading.

- **Seed rounds are getting structurally larger.** Glow's $180M seed, Neo's $25M seed, and ASIO's $15M seed continue the 2026 trend of compressed funding timelines and inflated early-stage rounds. Companies are raising 12-18 months of runway at seed to skip traditional milestones.

- **AgriTech sees strategic capital.** Plantopia's round led by Schreiber Foods (a $7B+ dairy company) represents the type of strategic-led deal that accelerates commercialization. Corporate investors leading at seed in agri/foodtech is a positive signal for the sector's maturation.

- **Repeat founders drive outsized rounds.** Glow (Meta, Snowflake, Claroty alumni), Neo (SentinelOne, Wiz, PANW alumni), Cordant (Rapyd alumni), and Elio (Meta/Pebbles alumni) all feature teams with significant prior exits or leadership at scaled companies. Investor appetite for Israeli repeat founders remains extremely strong.
