# Menomadin Investments Weekly Deal Report

**Week of September 21 -- September 28, 2026**

Deal flow slowed significantly this week as the Jewish High Holidays (Rosh Hashanah) overlapped with Q3's close. Three confirmed venture rounds totaling $65 million were announced, alongside an $87 million Israel-focused secondary fund. The week's activity skewed toward AI-enabled verticals: legal tech, cardiac imaging, and wildfire response. In ecosystem news, Bessemer Venture Partners closed $5.75 billion across two new funds with explicit plans to accelerate Israeli dealmaking.

---

## Section 1: Top Deals

**Chamelio** | Series A | $26M
[MANDATE FIT: AI]
- What they do: AI-native platform for in-house legal teams that automates contract review, negotiation, and workflow management. Replaces legacy contract lifecycle management software with agents that can draft, redline, and execute legal documents.
- Investors: Entree Capital (lead), Work-Bench, Emerge Ventures, Bright Pixel Capital
- Why it matters: Annual recurring revenue grew fourfold in eight months since seed. Customers include Wiz, monday.com, Socure, AppsFlyer, and Wonderful. The $26M Series A sits above the 92nd percentile for legal-tech Series A rounds in the US.
- Signals: Founded by Alex Zilberman (ex-Pagaya, co-founder of AnyVision) and Gil Banyas (ex-GC at AnyVision). Repeat founder team with deep enterprise sales experience. Valuation reportedly in the "hundreds of millions."
- Suggested angle: AI-native B2B SaaS with rapid revenue traction and a repeat founder team. Catalyst mandate should track for Series B participation if the growth trajectory holds. Legal AI is an underpenetrated vertical with strong enterprise pull.

**UltraSight** | Series B2 | $24M
[MANDATE FIT: HealthTech]
- What they do: AI-guided cardiac ultrasound platform that enables trained clinical staff to perform focused echocardiography with real-time AI guidance. FDA 510(k) cleared. Currently active across more than 10 commercial sites in the US.
- Investors: Alive HealthTech Growth Fund (lead), Deep Insight, Star51 Capital, Connecticut Innovations, eHealth Ventures. Existing investors Mayo Clinic Ventures, NYU, Iron Nation
- Why it matters: Addresses a critical shortage of cardiac imaging specialists. Published clinical validation in JAMA Cardiology. The platform bridges point-of-care diagnostics with specialist-level cardiac imaging, expanding access in underserved settings.
- Signals: Founded 2018 by Itay Kezurer, Prof. Yaron Lipman (Weizmann Institute), and Dr. Achi Ludomirsky. HQ in Cambridge, MA with R&D in Rehovot. Total funding now $42M. Mayo Clinic's continued participation signals clinical validation.
- Suggested angle: Strong Impact mandate fit. Series B2 pricing may be accessible for a co-investment. HealthTech meets accessibility: AI-guided imaging can democratize cardiac diagnostics in resource-limited environments. Worth a direct outreach this week.

**Prime Minute** | Pre-Seed | $15M
[MANDATE FIT: CleanTech/Energy]
- What they do: Precision-guided aerial suppression technology for wildfire and natural disaster response. Developed Precision Guided Suppression Units (PGSU) for rapid, targeted initial assault on emerging fires. Tested in Colorado, Oregon, Israel, and Uganda.
- Investors: Eclipse (lead), 8VC
- Why it matters: $15M is an unusually large pre-seed, reflecting investor conviction around climate resilience infrastructure. Wildfires caused $50B+ in damages in the US alone in 2025. The team combines Israeli and US military veterans with Palantir and Forterra alumni.
- Signals: Emerged from stealth September 24. Dual-use technology with defense and civilian applications. Eclipse continues its thesis of backing Israeli founders building for the physical world (also led Noetive's $41M seed last week).
- Suggested angle: Pre-seed is early for Menomadin's check size, but the Impact mandate should track this closely. CleanTech/climate resilience is a priority sector, and Prime Minute's Israeli founding team and dual-use positioning align well. Monitor for seed or Series A in the next 12 months.

**Ibex Investors** | Secondary Fund | $87M
- What they do: US-based investment firm specializing in Israel-focused venture capital and secondary transactions. The new fund (Fund II) purchases shares from founders, employees, and early investors in growth-stage Israeli companies, providing liquidity before IPO or acquisition.
- Fund manager: Osnat Velingard (ex-Susquehanna Growth Equity)
- Why it matters: Growing secondary market activity signals ecosystem maturation. As Israeli startups stay private longer, liquidity solutions become critical for retaining talent and recycling capital. Ibex's first secondary fund was $40M (2024); the step-up to $87M reflects strong LP demand.
- Signals: Ibex also manages a $106M primary venture fund. The firm is bridging US capital markets with Israeli growth companies at a time when IPO windows remain uncertain.

## Section 3: Market Insights

- **Holiday-driven slowdown.** The Jewish High Holidays (Rosh Hashanah, September 22-24) compressed deal activity. Only three venture rounds were confirmed this week, the lightest week since mid-August. Expect a backlog of announcements in early October as the market reopens after Yom Kippur (October 1).

- **Bessemer doubles down on Israel.** Bessemer Venture Partners closed $5.75 billion across two funds on September 23: $1.75B for seed/early-stage and $4B for growth. The firm explicitly signaled plans to accelerate its pace of Israeli investments, having backed approximately 80 Israeli startups since 2007 (more than half at seed). This capital injection will have downstream effects on Israeli deal pricing and competition for allocation.

- **AI-for-verticals thesis gaining momentum.** Two of the three deals this week (Chamelio for legal, UltraSight for cardiac imaging) represent AI applied to specific professional verticals rather than horizontal infrastructure. This pattern continues the broader 2026 trend of investor appetite shifting from AI platform plays toward domain-specific AI applications with clear revenue models.

- **Physical-world AI emerging as a cluster.** Eclipse led both Prime Minute ($15M, disaster response) and last week's Noetive ($41M, industrial operations). The firm is building a concentrated thesis around AI applied to physical environments. This cluster is early-stage but growing fast, and it intersects with Menomadin's Impact mandate (CleanTech, Energy).

- **Secondary market deepening.** Ibex's $87M Fund II (up from $40M for Fund I) reflects growing demand for liquidity solutions in the Israeli ecosystem. With IPO markets still selective and Israeli startups raising larger private rounds, secondary transactions are becoming a structural feature of the market rather than an opportunistic play.

## Section 4: Special Flags

- **Repeat founders to watch.** Alex Zilberman (Chamelio) previously co-founded AnyVision (now Oosto) and held leadership roles at Pagaya. His co-founder Gil Banyas served as General Counsel at AnyVision. This is a team that has operated at scale before and understands enterprise sales cycles. The fourfold ARR growth since seed is worth monitoring.

- **High-priority opportunity: UltraSight.** This is the strongest mandate fit of the week. Series B2 pricing, HealthTech sector, accessibility thesis, Mayo Clinic validation, and Israeli R&D. The Impact mandate ($0.5M-$5M checks) could participate in a follow-on or co-investment. Recommend outreach to Alive HealthTech Growth Fund or directly to CEO Itay Kezurer this week.

- **Bessemer's Israeli portfolio as a competitive signal.** Bessemer named Onyx, Torq, Neo, DriveNets, Port, and Upwind as Israeli portfolio companies. Several of these operate in Catalyst mandate sectors (Cyber, AI, B2B SaaS). Bessemer's increased pace means they will be competing more aggressively for Israeli Series A allocations. Early relationship-building with their Tel Aviv team is worth prioritizing.

- **Post-holiday deal pipeline.** With Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur (October 1) bracketing this period, expect a concentrated burst of deal announcements in the first two weeks of October. Teams that delayed announcements during the holidays will push releases, and Q4 fundraising cycles will begin in earnest. This is a good window for proactive outreach before the market gets noisy again.
